# Situation: Greetings

## Learner goal
After completing this pack, the learner should be able to greet someone appropriately in Kirundi, adjusting for time of day, age of the person, and level of familiarity.

## Core communicative functions
- Greeting (morning, afternoon, evening)
- Asking how someone is
- Responding to "how are you"
- Saying goodbye
- Greeting an elder respectfully
- Greeting a friend casually
- Greeting a group

## Core vocabulary
- Mwaramutse (good morning)
- Mwiriwe (good afternoon/evening)
- Amakuru (how are you / what's the news)
- Ni meza (it's good / I'm fine)
- Bite (how is it — informal)
- Mushingantahe (respectful address for older man)
- Nyokuru (grandmother — respectful)
- (add more as you create content)

## Register notes
- Greeting elders requires respectful forms — this is not optional in Kirundi culture
- Among friends, greetings are shorter and more casual
- Greeting a group has specific patterns
- Time of day matters for greeting choice

## Cultural notes
- Greetings in Burundi are longer and more elaborate than in Western cultures
- Skipping greetings or rushing them can be perceived as rude
- Physical greetings (handshakes, sometimes embraces) accompany verbal greetings
- It's common to ask about family members during greetings
